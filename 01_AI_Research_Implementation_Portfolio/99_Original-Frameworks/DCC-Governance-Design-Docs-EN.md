# Overview Document

---

## 0. Positioning of This Document

This document is an overview intended to organize the full picture of **“Declarative Closed-Loop Co-Creation Governance Design Theory”** in a form that readers involved in research, design, and implementation can **grasp in a short time**.

* **Principle Definition Document**: Fixes invariant conditions necessary for viability (principles to prevent breakdown)
* **Integrated Philosophy Definition Document**: Fixes decision criteria (what to prioritize and how to converge)
* **Overview Document (this document)**: Provides a bird’s-eye view of the overall structure, scope of application, operational image, and expected deliverables
* **Theory System Document**: Organizes concepts, structures, and relationships as a system
* **Operational Process System Document**: Describes the closed-loop operating procedures as state transitions
* **Governance Document**: Defines governance (constraints, disclosure, safety, inheritance, operational discipline)
* **Integrated Compendium Document**: Integrates the above into a single volume for easy reference
* **Addendum**: Separates formalization notes / extension theories (multi-LLM, etc.) from the main text

The purpose of this document is not “detailed explanation,” but to clarify **what to treat this design theory as, and how to use it**.

---

## 1. What This Tries to Solve (Problem Statement)

Design and research using generative AI are difficult to assetize due to the following problems.

* Conversations can look like outcomes, and **no deliverables (documents/design/verification) remain**
* Assertions, assumptions, and vague terms get mixed in, and later it becomes **unclear what is confirmed and what is hypothetical**
* Branches keep increasing without integration, resulting in a **pile of fragments with no reusability**
* It fails to connect to implementation/verification, falling into a state where **only explanations improved**
* Constraints (safety, disclosure, operations) are added afterward, requiring major rework later

This design theory provides a framework that avoids these issues while making dialogue with AI work as **“a closed loop that increases design assets.”**

---

## 2. Summary of This Design Theory (Conclusion First)

**Declarative Closed-Loop Co-Creation Governance Design Theory** is a design theory for satisfying the following.

1. Turn generative outputs produced through dialogue with AI into **deliverables (assets)**
2. Make deliverables **structured and verifiable**, and re-inject them as the next input (materials)
3. Not merely increase branches, but reach **integration, convergence, and fixed points**
4. Prevent error amplification by embedding **damping (brakes)** within the closed loop
5. Embed **governance** into the design from the beginning to enable sustainable operation

This design theory is not conversation technique or prompt craft itself, but is defined as a **deliverable-centered design process**.

---

## 3. Minimal Understanding of Terms (Only What This Document Requires)

Detailed definitions are deferred to the Principle Definition Document; this document records only the minimum necessary for overview understanding.

* **Co-creation**: An act in which humans and AI incorporate each other’s outputs as materials, expand the design space of deliverables, and move toward integration and fixation
* **Closed loop**: A structure in which deliverables are re-injected as inputs for the next generation, and states, deliverables, verification, and termination conditions are managed
* **Deliverables**: Documents / structures / implementations / evidence that remain in a form that can be referenced, reused, and verified later
* **Materials**: Inputs such as design docs, discussion logs, observed facts, casual chat content, fragment notes, etc.
* **Governance**: Constraints, operational discipline, disclosure policy, and inheritance design to avoid misuse, breakdown, and backtracking

---

## 4. Applicable Targets and Non-Targets (To Prevent Misunderstanding)

### 4.1 Applicable Targets

This design theory applies to activities such as the following.

* Generate new concepts/theories/specs/architectures via dialogue with AI, **systematize and fix** them, and drop them into implementation and verification
* **Integrate without contradiction** structures spanning multiple perspectives (design, research, implementation, operations, ethics, safety)
* Accumulate generative outputs not as “one-off outputs,” but as **inheritable design assets**

### 4.2 Non-Targets (What This Design Theory Does Not Primarily Aim At)

* One-off ideation, one-off code generation
* Efficiency of task delegation (so-called “high-performance IDE” use only)
* Emotional care or casual chat itself (however, chat content may become materials)

---

## 5. Increasing “Deliverables,” Not Just Generative Outputs (Types of Deliverables)

Deliverables that should be increased through closed-loop co-creation can be broadly organized into the following categories.

1. **Definition-type**: Terms, boundaries, assumptions, falsification conditions
2. **Structure-type**: Layered structures, module partitioning, dependencies, interfaces
3. **Procedure-type**: Process definitions, state transitions, verification points, fixed-point conditions
4. **Verification-type**: Test perspectives, evaluation metrics, experiment logs, failure logs
5. **Governance-type**: Constraints, disclosure scope, prohibitions, operational discipline, inheritance methods
6. **Evidence-type**: Diffs, decision logs, reasons for adoption/rejection, reference links (internal references)

The “goodness” of a conversation tends to be subjective, while deliverables can be made more objective.
This design theory places the increase of deliverables at the center of progress.

---

## 6. Overall Flow (Operational Image: High Level)

The movement of this design theory can be roughly expressed as the following cycle (details are in the Operational Process System Document).

1. **Material injection**: Aggregate materials (logs, memos, observations, past deliverables)
2. **Exploratory generation**: AI presents branch candidates, falsification candidates, formalization candidates
3. **Integration**: Organize duplication, contradictions, and dependencies, and reduce to the core
4. **Fixation**: Fix definitions, boundaries, interfaces, and falsification conditions
5. **Verification connection**: Drop into minimum implementation / minimum experiments, leaving evidence
6. **Re-injection**: Return results (success, failure, constraints) as materials to the next loop
7. **Termination**: Close the design phase when fixed-point conditions are met

What matters is not “generating,” but that **integration → fixation → verification → re-injection** is closed.

---

## 7. Intent to Separate Tiers (Reality Line / Theory Line / Currently Impossible Line)

In this design theory, Tier separation is performed from the beginning to make discussions viable.
This is not to deny possibility, but to **avoid breakdown caused by conflation**.

* **Tier1: Sufficiently feasible with current technology**

  * Can connect immediately to verification/implementation
* **Tier2: Logically possible**

  * Attractive, but requires ingenuity to connect to verification
* **Tier3: Unestablished / unobserved even in theory**

  * Do not fix (hold as hypotheses and avoid excessive assertion)

Tier separation is operated as an “organizing axis” for integration and verification connection.

---

## 8. Why It Is Declarative (Overview)

To say this design theory is “declarative” means that instead of fixing procedures in fine detail, it declares:

* **States that must be preserved**
* **Constraints that must be satisfied**
* **Fixed-point conditions that must be reached**

and within that scope, explores and integrates optimal pathways.

Procedural details vary by situation, but if states and constraints are fixed, the process becomes robust against fluctuation.
This point is made more concrete in the Governance Document.

## 10. Expected Effects (What Improves)

Applying this design theory is expected to yield at least the following.

* Deliverables accumulate not as “fragments” but as **reusable assets**
* Confusion between “confirmed/hypothesis” and “premise/conclusion” decreases, suppressing **error amplification**
* Because integration and fixed points are included in the design, it is less likely to become **an endless discussion**
* Since connection to verification is assumed, the **speed of dropping into implementation/experiments** increases
* Since governance is included from the beginning, **backtracking costs** in disclosure and operations are less likely to grow

---

## 11. Current Issues (Overview Level)

When dropping this design theory into real operation, the following issues are anticipated at an overview level.

* **Cost of deliverable-ization**: Work is required to drop conversations into assets
* **Burden of integration**: The more branches increase, the more time integration takes
* **Lack of verification design**: If falsification conditions and evaluation metrics are weak, the loop can easily become self-satisfying
* **Difficulty of Tier operation**: A pathway is needed to bring Tier2 down into Tier1
* **Over/under governance**: If constraints are thin, breakdown is more likely; if thick, exploration may become emaciated

These are handled concretely in subsequent documents (especially the Operational Process System Document / Governance Document).
